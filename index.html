ADA series: three new looks

Each direction is shown as four real 1920 × 1080 frames from the script: the series title card, the roadmap, a section title and the deadline. In plays a frame's entrance, Out plays its exit.

  • Things slide on and off; no fades
  • One move at a time
  • Horizontal and vertical are separate moves
  • Lines and boxes slide or grow, never both at once
  • Boxes never turn; text may turn 90° on a corner, never over other text
  • Icons draw themselves, one part at a time
  • No bounce

Semaphore

Named after railway signal arms. Each heading wraps into short lines, arrives hanging on end, reading downward, then swings 90° up on its bottom-left corner to lie level and read, like a signal arm raised to "go". Colour blocks never turn: they grow up from a baseline or out from an edge. Icons draw themselves part by part. The frame is built like a machine, one part at a time.

Why accessibility matters

Some use a screen reader that speaks the page aloud.

Archivo in its wide cut for headlines: square, engineered and calm. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (designed by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers) for everything else.

Icons

Motion

  • Headings: wrapped to a short measure, they arrive hanging on end, slide up into place, then swing 90° up on the bottom-left corner to lie level. Only headings turn; labels and body text slide.
  • A heading swings before anything above it appears, so it never crosses other text.
  • Blocks never turn: they grow up from a baseline or out from an edge. Lines only grow in length.
  • Highlight: marigold grows across the tile, then sweeps away to the right. Labels slide out once and stay put.
  • Icons draw themselves part by part: lines grow, then attached parts slide out from behind them, like the flag.

Daylight

Los Angeles light. Every scene sits on a sky gradient that shifts hue with the story: night blue into teal for the opening, into pine for the roadmap, into a dusk rust for the deadline. Big warm serif type, hairlines, and one gold line that grows under whatever matters. Nothing turns.

Focus

The video moves the way a keyboard user moves through a page. A warm Tab key is pressed, and a blue → green highlight jumps to the next item. A quiet line at the bottom reads out what a screen reader would say. The subject of the series becomes its visual language.
